# Facebook group post ideas that bring Page followers

Page: https://www.facebook.com/prositeguide/

## How this works (read first)
- **Groups hate links and sales posts.** Most posts below have NO link. The goal is replies and profile/Page visits, not clicks.
- **Post as your Page when the group allows it** (Group → "Interact as" ProSiteGuide). Everyone who sees a helpful Page post sees a "Follow" button next to the name. If the group only allows personal profiles, post as Jayce and make sure your profile's "Works at ProSiteGuide" links to the Page.
- **Reply to every comment within the first hour.** That's what makes Facebook show the post to more members, and it's where people decide to follow you.
- **The follow invite goes in your replies, not the post.** After someone engages, reply with help and end with something like: *"I post a short tip like this every week on my Page if it's useful: facebook.com/prositeguide"*. Only do this once or twice per post.
- **Links:** only drop an article link in a reply when someone asks for more detail, and only if the group rules allow links. Never post affiliate links in groups.
- **Rhythm:** 1 group post a day at most, in different groups. Don't paste the same post in 5 groups on the same day (Facebook flags it as spam). Rotate the ideas.
- **Best groups:** small business owners, entrepreneurs, freelancers, bloggers, WordPress help, Shopify/WooCommerce sellers, local business groups. Read each group's rules first.

## 1. Question posts (best for comments)

**Domains**
1. Quick question for business owners 👇 Did you buy your domain yourself, or did your web designer buy it for you? (Asking because I keep meeting owners who don't actually have the login to their own domain.)
2. If you could start your business again, would you pick a .com or something like .co, .shop, .studio? Why?
3. Has your domain ever expired by accident? What happened? Asking for a list of "lessons learned" I'm putting together.
4. Where did you register your domain: GoDaddy, Namecheap, Google/Squarespace, your host, somewhere else? Happy with them at renewal time?

**Hosting**
5. How much do you pay per month for your website hosting? And do you know what it renews at? (Most people are surprised by this one.)
6. Has your website ever gone down and you only found out from a customer? How long was it down?
7. Cheap shared hosting or managed hosting: which are you on, and would you switch?
8. If your hosting company disappeared tomorrow, do you have a backup of your website that you could restore? Be honest 😅

**WordPress / website builders**
9. WordPress, Wix, Squarespace or Shopify: what's your website built on, and what's the one thing you'd change about it?
10. What's the most confusing part of running your own website? I'll answer as many as I can in the comments.
11. How many plugins does your WordPress site have? (Over 30? Let's talk 😄)
12. When did you last update your website's plugins and theme? Today / this month / no idea.
13. Did you build your website yourself or hire someone? Would you do it the same way again?

**Security**
14. Has your website or business email ever been hacked? What tipped you off?
15. Does everyone on your team share one login for the website or social accounts? (No judgment, it's really common. There's an easy fix.)
16. Do you use a password manager for your business? Which one?

**Speed and Google**
17. Have you ever checked how fast your website loads on a phone? Run it through PageSpeed Insights and drop your mobile score below. I'll tell you the top fix for the lowest few.
18. When someone Googles your business name, what shows up first: your website, your Google Business Profile, or a directory like Yelp?
19. Do you know how many people visited your website last month? Yes / roughly / no idea.

**Email**
20. Do you use a Gmail/Yahoo address for your business or an email @yourdomain? Has it ever cost you a client?
21. Have your business emails ever landed in clients' spam folders? (It's usually 3 missing settings called SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Happy to explain.)

**Costs**
22. What did you pay for your current website: under $500, $500–2,000, $2,000–5,000, or more? Was it worth it?
23. What's one website or software subscription you pay for that you're not sure you need?

## 4. Quick-tip posts (value first, follow at the end)
End each one with: *"I share one tip like this every week on my Page, ProSiteGuide, if you'd like more."* (Only if the group allows mentioning your Page.)

31. **Domain tip:** Log in to your domain account today and check 3 things: auto-renew is ON, the email on the account is one you still use, and 2-factor login is ON. Losing a domain is one of the most expensive website mistakes and it's 100% avoidable.
32. **Hosting tip:** Before you buy hosting, look for the renewal price, not the first-year price. The "$2.99/month" deal often renews at 3–5x that.
33. **Backup tip:** A backup only counts if you've tested restoring it once. Ask your host how you'd restore your site if it broke today.
34. **WordPress tip:** Delete plugins and themes you're not using (deactivating isn't enough). Every unused plugin is something that can be hacked.
35. **Security tip:** Stop sharing one admin login. Give each person their own login with only the access they need. When someone leaves, you remove one user instead of changing every password.
36. **Speed tip:** The #1 thing slowing most small business sites is a giant image at the top of the homepage. Resize it to about 2,000px wide and compress it. It can cut load time a lot.
37. **Email tip:** If your emails go to spam, ask whoever manages your domain to set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC. It's a 15-minute job and fixes most deliverability problems.
38. **Google tip:** Your Google Business Profile often matters more than your website for local customers. Add photos, your hours and reply to every review.
39. **Ownership tip:** Make sure YOU own your domain, hosting and website accounts, not your web designer. If they disappear, you shouldn't lose your website.
40. **Builder tip:** Wix and Squarespace are easier to start; WordPress gives you more control and is easier to move later. Neither is "wrong": pick based on how much you want to manage.
